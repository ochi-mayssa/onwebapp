{% trans "Concept 1" %}

{% trans "What Is Workflow Automation?" %}

{% trans "Workflow automation focuses on business processes. It coordinates a sequence of tasks and activities so that work moves from one step to the next with less manual chasing. When a step is completed, the workflow can request an approval, send a notification, apply a condition or pass the work to another team." %}

{% trans "It is centred on how people and business applications interact: forms, decisions, approvals and handovers. The goal is to make everyday processes more consistent, reduce waiting time and keep information aligned across the departments involved." %}

{% trans "Concept 2" %}

{% trans "What Is Workload Automation?" %}

{% trans "Workload automation is about executing technical jobs and workloads automatically. It schedules and orchestrates jobs across systems, respects dependencies between them and monitors whether each run succeeds. If something fails, it can retry, alert an operator or follow a defined recovery path." %}

{% trans "It is centred on computers rather than people: servers, databases, applications and data pipelines. The goal is to make recurring technical processing dependable, timely and observable." %}

{% trans "What it typically involves" %}

  • {% trans "Automated execution of technical jobs and workloads" %}

{% trans "Concept 3" %}

{% trans "What Is Workload Management?" %}

{% trans "Workload management is different again. It is not about automating a process or a job — it is about planning, assigning, distributing and monitoring work. It answers the human and organisational questions: what needs to be done, who is responsible, how much capacity is available and how work is progressing." %}

{% trans "Where workload automation runs jobs on a schedule, workload management organises tasks, projects and responsibilities. It helps teams avoid overload, balance distribution and keep priorities visible across projects and departments." %}

{% trans "What it focuses on" %}

  • {% trans "Planning work" %}

  • {% trans "Organizing projects" %}
  • {% trans "Balancing team workloads" %}
  • {% trans "Monitoring progress" %}
  • {% trans "Managing capacity and priorities" %}

{% trans "Is workflow automation the same as workload automation?" %}+

{% trans "No. Workflow automation automates business processes — sequences of tasks, approvals, notifications and handovers between people and applications. Workload automation automates the execution of technical jobs and workloads, such as scheduled processing, backups and ETL pipelines. They address different parts of an operation." %}

{% trans "What is the difference between workload management and workload automation?" %}+

{% trans "Workload automation runs technical jobs automatically according to schedules and dependencies. Workload management organises people and work: planning tasks, assigning responsibilities, organising projects, balancing capacity and monitoring progress. One executes jobs; the other manages work." %}

{% trans "Can workflow automation and workload automation work together?" %}+

{% trans "Yes. They are often complementary. A business workflow may depend on background jobs that workload automation runs and monitors, and the outcome of a job can feed back into a workflow as the next step or trigger." %}

{% trans "Which solution does a business need?" %}+

{% trans "It depends on the problem. If the challenge is a business process with approvals and handovers, workflow automation is usually the starting point. If the challenge is recurring technical processing, workload automation is more relevant. If the challenge is organising people, tasks and capacity, workload management is the right focus — and the three are frequently combined." %}
